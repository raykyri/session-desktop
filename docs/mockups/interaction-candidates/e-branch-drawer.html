This candidate

Candidate E opens branches in a drawer that overlays the right part of the column area. Opening, replacing or closing a branch does not change the width, position or two-column layout of the feed or the conversation behind it.

  • What changed. A branch opened from a passage, the branch menu, the inline list, a feed child row or the sibling arrows appears in the drawer: 46% of the column area, 380–640px wide, full height. Its header has the breadcrumb, sibling arrows, star, Pin as column and Close; its turns are always stacked. A nested branch, or another branch from the parent, replaces the drawer content, and the breadcrumb goes back up. Esc or Close closes it and returns focus to the passage that opened it. Clicks in the columns behind it do not close it. Below 620px the drawer covers the column area and has a Back button.
  • Pinned columns. Pin as column moves the branch into a 560px column appended to the right. Column widths depend only on the window width (feed 240–320px, the root conversation takes the rest), so the row scrolls horizontally when pinned columns don't fit. The focus-budget solver, spines and group spines are removed; focusing a column only scrolls it into view.
  • Motion removed. The feed no longer shrinks from 320px to 260px, the parent no longer shrinks from 862px to 300px and switches to stacked, focusing a column no longer swaps widths, and nested branches no longer reflow or collapse other columns.
  • Costs. The drawer covers most of the parent's answer column at 1440px (only the question column stays visible at 1100px), so passages under it can't be clicked until it closes; the branch menu beside each question still works. When the passage is under the drawer and its question is out of view, the parent scrolls that question to the top. Pinning scrolls the row horizontally, which moves the feed out of view and cuts off the left of the parent. Branch and parent can't be read side by side in two-column layout.

Summary

The proposal turns the app into one horizontal stack of columns: feed, conversation, branch, branch of a branch. Questions in the feed can be filed into Drafts, Archive, and user folders by dragging. Each conversation is a two-column grid with questions on the left and answers on the right, and a column switches to a stacked layout when it gets narrow. Because of that switch, nesting can go to any depth without running out of width.

The column layout is a Focus stack: the focused column gets the most width and ancestor columns collapse into spines, in the style of Andy Matuschak's notes. Equal-width Miller columns (Tiled) and nesting branches inline were also considered; the options table below gives the reasons for choosing the focus stack. The left sidebar can collapse to a 52px icon strip, and the traffic lights get a reserved zone that stays fixed through every sidebar and fullscreen state.

Review of the current app

Findings from the screenshots and the code (App.tsx, ResearchColumns.tsx, ResearchDocument.tsx, shell.css, tauri.conf.json).

  • You can't read a branch next to its parent. Branches are cards in a 220–260px rail beside the answer. Opening one replaces the document with the branch's own spine, plus a Back link. To go two levels deep you have to follow Back links, and nothing shows where you are in the tree.
  • The layout has only two panes. ResearchColumns splits the window into a feed column and a content column, and below 880px it shows one. Nothing gives a third column room.
  • Long threads read as one tall scroll. Collapsing works on the whole transcript ("Show full transcript"), not per turn. In a four-turn thread like the Richard Ngo summary, the questions end up screens apart, and there is no overview of the turns.
  • Questions can't be filed. Client-side folders were removed in d51a8d4, although the backend storage for them was kept. Archive is a separate view, not a place you can drop a question. In the first screenshot, a post titled "Drafts" is being used as a folder, which shows the need.
  • Feed cards repeat their controls. Every card shows both a Follow text button and a bookmark, so the controls compete with the titles. Follow has no behavior yet beyond storing a flag (set_research_tree_followed).
  • A collapsed sidebar can strand you. Collapsing sets its width to 0. On Home, Bookmarks, and Archived no button brings it back; only ⇧⌘G and the command palette do.
  • The traffic lights overlap content. The window uses the Overlay title bar without setting trafficLightPosition. With the sidebar collapsed, the lights cover the document header's back and forward buttons, and there is no drag region outside the sidebar. Fullscreen isn't handled, so the 32px top padding stays even when macOS hides the lights.
  • The sidebar toggle moves. It sits at the right edge of a resizable sidebar (208–420px), so its position depends on the sidebar's width. After you collapse the sidebar, the button you just clicked is somewhere else.

The column model

Every column is one thing: the feed, or one conversation. A conversation is a chain of turns, which maps to inlineChainFor(node) in today's code. A branch is a non-inline child node with a queryAnchor; it opens as the next column to the right, never inside the parent.

Nesting rules

  • The columns show one path. They always run from the root to one leaf of the tree, as in Miller columns. Opening a different branch from column k replaces columns k+1 onward. Nothing is lost: every branch stays anchored in its parent's answer, and the anchor reopens it.
  • Siblings have a switcher. When a turn has several branches, the branch header shows 1/3 with previous and next buttons. The branch button next to the question shows how many branches the answer has and lists them all.
  • Depth has no limit, and width cost stays bounded. Columns that don't fit collapse to 44px spines, each showing its depth and a running indicator. At most three spines show on each side. When there are more, the outermost ones merge into a single spine labelled with a count (for example, +3), and clicking it lists the hidden columns. A path of any depth costs at most 132px of spines on each side.
  • The parent shows where you are. Opening a branch expands the parent turn it came from and scrolls the parent column so that turn's question is at the top; the anchor is in the answer beside it. "Jump to passage" scrolls to the anchor itself. Passages with branches are always tinted blue; while a branch is open its passage is also underlined and the count on the turn's branch button turns blue. This matches the selected row in a Miller column.
  • The turn grid responds to its column, not the window. A container query switches a turn from stacked to question | answer at 600px of column width. The same component renders correctly at any depth, so a two-column grid never ends up inside another two-column grid. This is what makes the recursion work.
  • Focus only moves when you ask it to. Clicking a spine, a column header, a breadcrumb, an anchor, or a branch in the fork menu moves focus, and so do [ and ]. The focused column has a 2px accent rule along its top edge. Clicking or selecting text inside a column does not, so the layout never shifts under a selection.
  • Empty branches are thrown away. A branch is saved when its first question is sent. If you close it before that, it is discarded and no orphaned anchors are left behind.
  • Highlights work as before. They stay as character offsets within one block, as with ResearchHighlightAnchor today. A highlight can be turned into a branch, and an anchored passage can carry a highlight too.

Width budget

Every column has a minimum and a preferred width. First, every column is given its minimum. Any width left over goes first to the focused column, up to its preferred width, then to conversation neighbours nearest first, then to the feed. Whatever remains goes to the focused column. A column collapses to a spine only when the minimums don't fit, starting with the column farthest from focus; at equal distance the feed goes first.

Feed cards

  • Only the question. A card shows the question, up to 4 lines, with the thread title above it on up to two lines when the title differs. There is no metadata row: no timestamp, follow-up count, draft badge, or inline bookmark button. Unfiled questions have no section heading.
  • Status as a dot. Running, failed, and unsent questions show a coloured dot in the card's top-right corner (pulsing for running). It's replaced by the menu button on hover and is announced to screen readers with the question.
  • Composer. No keyboard hint text. The primary button (Ask, Send, or the arrow) shows a ↵ glyph only while its field is focused and has text, which is exactly when Enter would submit. Enter in an empty field does nothing; Shift+Enter adds a line.
  • Scrollbar. The feed's scrollbar takes no width: a thin thumb is drawn over the right edge on hover and while scrolling. In the app, WKWebView already uses overlay scrollbars unless "Show scroll bars: Always" is set in System Settings; to keep the feed's width fixed in that case too, hide the WebKit scrollbar on the feed and draw the same overlay thumb.
  • One menu. The "…" button at the top right opens a menu with Bookmark or Remove bookmark at the top, then Move to.

Conversation turns

  • Questions on the left, answers on the right. The question column is 40% of the turn, with a 180px minimum. Questions scroll with their answers; nothing in the turn grid is sticky.
  • Answers start collapsed. A finished answer is clamped to 9 lines and fades out at the bottom. Clicking the faded edge expands it; a "Show more" label appears there on hover. Answers that fit get no fade and no dots.
  • Length dots are the toggle. A rail beside a long answer shows one mark per clamped height of content, with the first mark filled while the answer is clamped. Clicking the rail expands the answer; it stays visible with all marks grey while the answer is open, and clicking it again collapses the answer. The rail is a button with aria-expanded, so it is also the keyboard control; the fade is a pointer target only.
  • Turn actions. Next to each question's time are its star and branch buttons. Both appear on hover (a filled star stays visible). The branch button shows how many branches the answer has and opens a menu of them with "New branch here"; with no branches it starts one. Selecting text gives Highlight, Branch, and Copy. Clicking a highlighted or anchored passage opens its branch, or a menu when there are several.
  • The follow-up composer belongs to its column. Each conversation column has its own composer, the equivalent of today's "Continue thread", so you can ask in a parent without leaving the branch. It sits after the last turn and scrolls with the conversation; it is not pinned to the bottom.

Folders and dragging

  • Where folders appear. The Home view lists unfiled questions first, without a heading, then a tray for each folder (Drafts, user folders, then Archive), as in the second screenshot. The same folders are in the sidebar, or as icons in the strip. "Home" is the view and "Unfiled" is the place: a question is never "in Home".
  • Drop targets. You can drop a question on a tray, on a sidebar row, or on a strip icon (its label shows while you hover during a drag). The Home row is a drop target for Unfiled, so a question can go back from any view. Dropping between cards in a tray sets the order, shown by an insertion line.
  • Spring-loading. Hovering over a collapsed tray for 650ms during a drag opens it, so you can place the card precisely.
  • Same-folder drops do nothing. Dropping on the folder a question is already in shows a no-drop cursor, and no toast appears.
  • Undo. Every move to another folder shows a toast with Undo for 6 seconds. Reordering within a folder doesn't show one.
  • Asking from a folder view. A question asked from a user folder's view is filed in that folder. From Drafts or Archive it goes to Unfiled.
  • Naming folders. New folder and Rename open a small modal dialog over the window, with the field focused. Enter or the primary button confirms; Esc, Cancel, or a click outside closes it.
  • Move to. Use a card's "…" menu, or the folder button in a conversation header, to open "Move to". It lists every folder, marks the current one as disabled, and ends with "New folder…", which creates the folder and moves the question in one step.
  • Archive. Archive maps to archivedAt. An archived question is listed only in the Archive tray (Bookmarks still lists it if it is bookmarked) and stops sending follow-up notifications; its Follow button is disabled and says why. A running question moved to Archive keeps running, and the toast says so. An open conversation stays open and gets an Archived label.
  • Drafts. Drafts is a new system folder. The composer's "Save draft" puts a question there; the unused GlobalDraft type already describes a drafts surface. It accepts any question, sent or not, like any other folder. Sending a draft from Drafts moves it to Unfiled. There is no Undo here, because the question has already been sent.
  • User folders. These restore the client-side folders removed in d51a8d4, and should be stored in the backend that was kept. Names must be unique, ignoring case, and "Home" and "Unfiled" are reserved. Deleting a folder needs confirmation, and its questions move to Unfiled. Drafts and Archive can't be renamed or deleted.

Sidebar strip and traffic lights

  • Pin the lights. Set trafficLightPosition so the lights are vertically centred in a 44px header row, which every column header shares. Start from {"x": 16, "y": 16} and adjust on a real device. Today the lights sit in a 30px strip above the sidebar header.
  • Reserve a zone for the lights. With the full sidebar, the zone at the window's top-left is 116px × 44px: it covers the lights (x 16–68) and the sidebar toggle beside them (x 80–108).
  • Hide the lights with the icon strip. When the sidebar collapses to the strip, the window's close, minimise and zoom buttons are hidden (standardWindowButton(.closeButton)?.isHidden = true and the same for the other two, from the AppKit support package; Tauri has no setting for it). The expand button takes their 44px row, so Home and the icons below it don't move between states, and there is no reserved zone or header inset. ⌘W, ⌘M and the window menu still work. A variant worth testing on a device: show the lights again while the pointer is over the strip's top cell.
  • The toggle moves between states. With the full sidebar it sits beside the lights at x 80; with the strip it is the strip's top cell. After collapsing, the pointer is about 40px from the expand button.
  • Inset whatever is under the zone. Any column whose header bar starts left of the zone's right edge gets a left inset of (zone width − x). This only applies with the full sidebar in a window, where the sidebar is wider than the zone, so in practice no column needs an inset.
  • Fullscreen. macOS hides the lights. With the strip nothing changes; with the full sidebar the toggle moves left to x 12, which is acceptable because entering fullscreen already moves everything.
  • Drag region. The whole 44px header row of every column is a drag region (data-tauri-drag-region on the bar, not on its buttons), and double-clicking it zooms the window. Today only the sidebar can be dragged.
  • The strip replaces collapsing to 0px. It keeps navigation, highlight counts, and folder drop targets one click away. Icons show tooltips on hover. User folders show the first letter of their name; the tooltip gives the full name, so two folders may share a letter.
  • Automatic strip. Below 900px of window width the sidebar becomes the strip automatically, without changing the saved preference. The expand button above Home is disabled there, and its tooltip says why.
  • No hover peek. A hover peek of the full sidebar over the strip was rejected. During a drag it would open whenever the pointer crossed the strip, and the strip icons already act as drop targets.

Promoted children

You can star an important follow-up so that it's treated as a child of its question. Hover a follow-up's question in a root conversation and click the star button (an outlined star; once starred it shows a filled star, both grey, with no text label); click it again to unstar. A branch is starred as a whole with the star button in its column header. In the data model this is one flag, for example promotedAt on a ResearchNode. Inline children are follow-ups and non-inline children are branches, so the same flag covers both.

Five ways of showing children were reviewed. In the conversation, starred follow-ups stay in line with the other turns (Flat); the indented and folded layouts below were tried and dropped.

Open questions

  • Should Follow move off the feed card entirely? This draft keeps it in the conversation header only, because Follow has no behavior yet.
  • Should a question be able to sit in Archive and in a user folder at the same time? This draft makes Archive exclusive, so a question is in one place at a time.
  • Post vs Ask in the composer: this draft covers Ask only. A network post would be a single-turn conversation without an answer column.
  • Swipe navigation with two fingers could move focus between columns instead of going back and forward through history.
